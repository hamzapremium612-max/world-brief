Pakistan at the SCO summit

Pakistan used its highest-profile multilateral appearance this year to stake out a position on shared water treaties while taking on a leadership role that will shape the bloc's agenda through 2027.

At the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation summit in Bishkek, Kyrgyzstan, Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif said water is the "lifeblood" of the region and must never be weaponised, and that treaties governing shared waters are solemn, binding commitments that must be honoured unconditionally. He said the region cannot realise its full potential without lasting peace. SCO leaders adopted the Bishkek Declaration to expand cooperation on security, trade and technology, and Pakistan took over the organisation's rotating chairmanship, meaning it will host the next Council of Heads of State meeting in 2027. The SCO separately named Lahore its Tourism and Cultural Capital for 2026-27.

Pakistan's western border under pressure

Pakistan is absorbing a renewed wave of border militancy and the human and administrative weight of mass Afghan returns at the same time, and both bear directly on already strained ties with Kabul.

Militants attempted to breach a Customs House in Balochistan on the night of August 31-September 1 in a coordinated attack; five security personnel and a customs official were martyred, and security forces killed 21 militants across the province in related operations in Chagai and Pishin districts. Separately, six million Afghans have now been forced to return to Afghanistan and start over under Taliban rule, with another million deported this year from Pakistan and Iran alone. Both episodes underline how unsettled the Pakistan-Afghanistan frontier remains, with militant violence and mass repatriation unfolding side by side.

Pakistan's economy under IMF pressure

These moves show Pakistan trying to satisfy IMF conditionality and rebuild investor confidence at the same time as it manages the domestic political cost of higher fuel prices.

Pakistan will phase out its Export Processing Zones and Special Economic Zones nationwide by 2035 under IMF Extended Fund Facility conditions, a parliamentary subcommittee was told, as the government moves toward a uniform tax regime. The government also launched a dual-tranche, dollar-denominated Eurobond with five- and ten-year tenors, seeking to return to international capital markets after recent sovereign rating upgrades. At the same time, it raised petrol and diesel prices again, and it is now negotiating with Jamaat-e-Islami over petroleum prices and levies, with the party demanding a "complete package" before formal talks begin.

Himalayan flooding and climate risk

The Nepal-Tibet disaster and the UN's warning both point to a growing, region-wide glacial flood risk that reaches into Pakistan's own northern areas, not just Nepal.

Flooding in Nepal and Tibet has killed more than 1,000 people, with rescue efforts continuing for hydropower workers caught by surging river water; five previously missing Australians have since been found alive. A UN official warned that the Himalayas face severe glacial flood risks in the years ahead, noting that researchers counted 47 dangerous glacial lakes across Nepal, China and India in 2020 and that the number has grown substantially since. Pakistan's National Disaster Management Authority separately warned of heavy rain and landslides across the country's northern areas and advised against unnecessary travel. Together, the warnings suggest this month's disaster is part of a wider glacial and monsoon flood risk building across the Himalayan region rather than an isolated event.

Iran-Israel war and the Middle East

The US-Israel war with Iran is still active and its diplomatic fallout is widening, from Chinese sanctions exposure to extended US naval deployments to renewed European involvement in the separate Gaza and West Bank crises.

The US-Israel war against Iran, which began with joint strikes on February 28, is continuing: US strikes reportedly killed four people at a wedding party, and Iranian state media say Iran has begun retaliatory missile and drone attacks on US targets. Chinese President Xi Jinping visited Egypt as both countries face the threat of US sanctions over financial links to Iran. In Gaza, Israel said it captured Hamas's internal security chief during a raid that killed four people, while in the West Bank, Britain vowed tougher measures against Israeli settler violence and Israel warned of retaliation if the UK imposes sanctions, with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu increasing support for settlements ahead of elections. Iranian President Pezeshkian criticised the UN's response to Israeli actions in Gaza and Lebanon but said Iran would reciprocate if the US returns to its commitments under an existing memorandum of understanding. The USS Abraham Lincoln's crew, whose deployment was extended because of the war with Iran, is now due a port visit in Thailand after more than 270 days at sea.

Trump's pressure campaign on US allies

Washington is simultaneously testing its leverage over a NATO ally, a G7 neighbour and a major Latin American democracy, a useful gauge of how far Trump is willing to push traditional US relationships.

Donald Trump suggested he is reviewing US support for Britain's sovereignty claim over the Falkland Islands, in comments seen as pressure on the UK to raise defence spending and NATO contributions. Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney told the US to "start being serious" amid an escalating trade war and is preparing to retaliate against US tariffs; Canada's ruling Liberals then swept three byelections, a result one MP called a message to Washington that Carney has the public's backing. Separately, the US was accused of meddling in another Brazilian election, with commentators drawing parallels to a US intervention in Brazil's politics 64 years earlier. Across the Falklands, Canada and Brazil, the administration is applying pressure on multiple US partners at once.
