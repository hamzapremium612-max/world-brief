Pakistan and the Gulf: the Makkah pact advances

Pakistan is formalising a trilateral defence and trade relationship with Saudi Arabia and Turkiye that could reshape its regional alignments.

Pakistan, Saudi Arabia and Turkiye held the first meeting of the Strategic Political and Defence Committee under the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement in Istanbul, with Deputy PM Dar, Defence Minister Khawaja Asif and the army chief representing Islamabad. The talks are meant to set up concrete defence coordination between the three states. Alongside this, Islamabad and Riyadh separately agreed to raise Pakistani agricultural and food exports to Saudi Arabia to $3 billion, naming rice, red meat, fruits and water-efficient farming technology as priority areas. Together the two tracks show Pakistan pursuing the Gulf relationship on both security and economic fronts at once.

Nepal-China border floods and the Grand Canyon flash flood

The Nepal disaster is one of the deadliest flood events in the region in years and sits close to Pakistan's own recurring flood exposure.

Floods near the Nepal-China border have killed at least 781 people in Nepal, with over 2,500 still missing five days into rescue efforts, while Chinese state media has confirmed 16 dead and hundreds missing in Tibet from the same event. A Nepali head teacher is credited with evacuating 900 children from a school minutes before the floodwaters hit, after receiving advance warning. Separately, flash flooding at the Grand Canyon left 15 hikers feared missing, with survivors describing the water rising with almost no warning.

The Nepal disaster echoes the recurring pattern of flash floods and glacial-lake outburst events across the Himalayan region, where heavy monsoon rainfall and rapid mountain runoff regularly overwhelm warning systems and infrastructure built for smaller, less frequent events. Trump's power plays: oil, borders and the press

Three unrelated stories from the same weekend show the same pattern: Trump using leverage, symbolism and regulatory threats to assert control, abroad and at home.

Trump hailed a deal giving the US control of 65 billion barrels of Venezuela's oil as historic, and Venezuela's interim president Delcy Rodriguez defended the arrangement domestically despite the outrage it has provoked. Trump also escalated a dispute with Canada over the renaming of Lake Ontario, posting a video of himself kicking over the Canadian sign, while Ontario's premier responded by erecting a giant sign of its own. Separately, Trump said a prominent NBC journalist should face punishment from federal broadcast regulators over coverage of his election endorsements.

Ferry disaster off northern Cyprus

A serious maritime accident with a rising death toll and an arrest of the crew for a possible safety failure.

A passenger ferry carrying nearly 270 people capsized and sank off northern Cyprus. At least eight people have died and around 18 remain missing, though 237 were rescued. Some survivors said an explosion occurred at the bow before the vessel rolled over and reported the ferry had an insufficient supply of life jackets. Police have taken the captain and crew into custody as authorities investigate.
