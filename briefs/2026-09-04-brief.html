Pakistan's Makkah Pact and regional defense shift

Pakistan is formalizing a tri-nation defense pact that reshapes its regional alliances just as the Middle East grows more volatile.

Pakistani officials described the Makkah Pact, a defense arrangement linking Pakistan with Saudi Arabia and Egypt, as an important strategic achievement that provides deterrence for peace and safeguards the sovereignty of all three states. Planning Minister Ahsan Iqbal called for an accompanying economic reset to sustain the gains, while a Lahore conference examined how the tri-nation agreement will affect regional peace and stability amid rapid geopolitical shifts. The pact signals Islamabad leaning further into a Gulf-anchored security bloc rather than relying only on its traditional partnerships. What follows will be whether the deterrence language turns into concrete military or economic commitments.

Pakistan's petroleum price fight

How the government manages fuel prices and street pressure will shape both household budgets and its relationship with opposition religious parties.

The government raised petrol and diesel prices again on Thursday, even as it moves toward deregulating fuel pricing entirely by next June, which would let the regulator adjust diesel rates without repeated cabinet approval. At the same time, it agreed with Jamaat-i-Islami to form committees aimed at recommending cuts to the petroleum levy, after JI-backed strikes shut down hundreds of Karachi markets and petrol pumps. Karachi's mayor criticized the strike, saying political parties should not impose economic hardship on citizens to make a point. The government is now negotiating relief on the levy at the same time it is preparing to give up direct control of pump prices altogether.

Pakistan's shadow finance under global scrutiny

Illicit finance channels are evolving faster than Pakistan's oversight, the same vulnerability that drove years of international scrutiny of the country.

A joint FATF-OECD report found that hawala and similar informal money transfer networks are increasingly using virtual assets and fintech platforms to conceal illicit wealth. Separately, Pakistan's National Assembly was told that illegally issued SIM cards continue to drive fraudulent calls and fake banking messages, even after regulators blocked 18.2 million illegal SIMs. Both point to the same weakness: shutting down one channel does not stop the money or the fraud, it just pushes them onto a newer, harder-to-trace platform.

El Nino intensifies, and Pakistan braces for floods

A global climate driver is now translating into an immediate flood warning for northern Pakistan.

Australia's Bureau of Meteorology says this El Nino has already broken records, with Pacific Ocean warming exceeding two degrees Celsius in six months, and the World Meteorological Organization has warned of a supersized El Nino that could disrupt economies worldwide. Pakistan's disaster management authority is now forecasting severe rain and potential flooding in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Gilgit-Baltistan and Azad Kashmir over the next 72 hours, warning that intense rainfall in the mountains could cause rivers, streams and seasonal nullahs to rise sharply. The two are directly connected: extreme ocean warming is feeding the kind of intense rainfall that now threatens Pakistan's mountainous north.

The Middle East's wars widen

The Iran-US conflict and the Israeli-Palestinian conflict are both escalating at once, and Washington is settling in for the long haul.

Iran attacked US military bases in Kuwait on Thursday despite Trump's threats of further strikes, deepening a war that is now six months old, and the US is investigating claims that a missile hit an Iranian wedding, killing four people. The Pentagon is extending American troop deployments across the Middle East into 2027, signaling it expects the conflict to drag on rather than resolve soon. In parallel, two Palestinian teenagers were killed during a settler attack on a West Bank village, and Israeli minister Itamar Ben-Gvir is reportedly planning to relocate 1.86 million Palestinians out of Gaza over seven years by offering destination countries, legal status, visas and housing. Together, the two fronts show a region where military and political entrenchment is replacing any near-term resolution.

Latin America reasserts itself

Argentina and Venezuela are both testing how far they can push against outside powers, one over territory and one over resources, while American capital and diplomacy push back into the region.

Argentina's president Javier Milei renewed his country's claim over the Falkland Islands, warning that oil companies operating in the British territory's waters could face sanctions and framing the moment as one where the winds of change favor Argentina. In Venezuela, Nobel laureate and opposition leader Maria Corina Machado argued that the country's government lacks the democratic legitimacy to negotiate an oil deal with the United States, part of a US-Venezuela relationship that has moved from cordial to what critics call predatory over the course of a century, centered on oil. Meanwhile, tech billionaire Peter Thiel's relocation to Argentina has drawn protests, with opponents saying laws before Argentina's congress would entrench the power of US tech billionaires in the country. The throughline is a region where old territorial claims and new alliances with foreign capital are both back in play.
