Pakistan's coalition under pressure

The ruling coalition's rifts over provinces and revenue are surfacing at the same time ordinary Pakistanis are absorbing new costs and the periphery says it is being ignored.

Pakistan's ruling coalition is fraying on two fronts. The PPP blocked two gas-revenue bills and demanded Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif clarify his silence on creating new provinces, while chants of 'Sindhu Desh Naa Manzoor' brought Sindh Assembly proceedings to a standstill. At the same time households are absorbing new costs: Nepra raised electricity tariffs by more than Rs45 billion, petrol and diesel prices moved in opposite directions, and the central bank had to publicly deny that its Raast payment system is linked to money laundering after an FATF report. Gilgit-Baltistan's chief minister separately told a Senate committee that poor connectivity is hampering tourism and disaster response in a region with 7,000 glaciers to monitor. The coalition's provincial disputes look set to continue unresolved even as the cost of living keeps rising.

Balochistan's security operations

The state is again relying on hard security and a reshuffled counter-terrorism command rather than a political settlement to manage Balochistan's unrest.

Pakistan's security apparatus was active on several fronts. ISPR said security forces killed 48 terrorists in high-tempo operations across Balochistan over 72 hours, destroying a cache of ammunition and improvised explosive devices in the process. Separately, the government reshuffled its counter-terrorism command, moving ISI's internal wing chief, Major General Faisal Naseer, to head Nacta. The Anti-Narcotics Force also said it had disrupted an Afghan drug-trafficking network, seizing 671kg of methamphetamine between Karachi Port and a joint operation in Colombo.

Iran's defiance and the Gulf standoff

Iran shows no sign of buckling six months into confrontation with the US, and Washington's pressure on Seoul over Hormuz suggests the standoff is spreading beyond the region.

US intelligence agencies now believe that six months of conflict have left Iran more confident in its ability to withstand American pressure, not less, according to a New York Times report, with Tehran reportedly determined to keep confronting US-linked targets in the Middle East. At the same time, Washington is pushing allies to share the burden of containing that standoff: South Korea is reviewing a possible deployment of military assets to the Strait of Hormuz under US pressure, though Seoul's presidential office is downplaying the reports.

The UN: symbolic reform and an unenforceable warning

The same institution that can correct a decades-old cartographic bias still has no way to act on its own warning that Israel's conduct in the West Bank may amount to crimes against humanity.

The UN took two very different actions this week. Its human rights office warned that Israel's forced displacement of the entire Palestinian population from three West Bank refugee camps, part of a military operation launched in January 2025, could amount to crimes against humanity, with more than 33,000 people already displaced. Separately, the General Assembly voted 164-1, with the United States the only country opposed, to adopt a new world map that shows Africa's true geographic scale and phases out the traditional Mercator projection. One move corrects a symbolic injustice; the other names a serious violation with no enforcement mechanism attached to it.

Falklands dispute reignites

A dispute most assumed was settled by a 1982 war is being revived by Trump's transactional approach to alliances, testing the durability of a postwar territorial settlement.

Argentina's president Javier Milei used a televised address to say the 'winds of change' now favour Argentina's claim to the Falkland Islands, citing signals that Donald Trump might reconsider decades of US support for British sovereignty over them. Milei also threatened swifter sanctions on UK oil interests operating near the islands. Britain rejected the claim immediately, with Downing Street and Ed Miliband both calling UK support for the Falklands 'unwavering.' The dispute is being revived in part because Trump has floated backing Milei's claim, reportedly as a favour to his ally and a swipe at the UK.

Russia's war and Europe's mounting strain

Continued Russian escalation and a spreading sabotage campaign are unfolding alongside stalled US-led peace efforts and a possible far-right breakthrough in German state politics.

Russia's war on Ukraine escalated again, with a drone strike hitting the security service's headquarters in Kyiv, which Ukraine's foreign minister called a 'major escalation.' Trump's peace envoys, Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, are due in Moscow and Kyiv this weekend, though talks have already stalled. Germany has blamed Russia for an attack on Leipzig airport as part of a wider, spiralling campaign of sabotage incidents across Europe. Separately, Germany's far-right AfD is contesting a state election in Saxony-Anhalt where an outright majority would give it its first taste of governing power since the Second World War.
