Pakistan's provincial power struggle and diplomatic balancing act

Pakistan's provincial rivalries are hardening into open street politics just as Islamabad manages sensitive trade and India relations abroad.

Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Chief Minister Sohail Afridi pushed his provincial rights campaign into Sindh, accusing the PPP of blocking his movement, asking the Punjab chief minister to apologise for remarks against other provinces, and vowing a street campaign in Karachi ahead of a September 27 mobilisation. PTI said police arrested party leader Hammad Azhar in Multan over a case tied to the May 2023 riots and called it political vengeance, while police said they had no knowledge of the arrest. The provincial friction comes as President Zardari's extended stay in London, alongside Interior Minister Naqvi who had floated creating new provinces, raised questions about the politics behind the trip, a proposal the PPP has rejected outright. Separately, Pakistan's Information Commission ruled that the National Assembly speaker cannot use a blanket classification to withhold employee records from the public unless it explains why disclosure would cause harm. On the foreign policy side, Islamabad is negotiating its reapplication for the EU's GSP+ trade scheme, with the EU envoy warning the benefits cannot be taken for granted, and the Foreign Office condemned the demolition of a century-old mosque in India's Uttar Pradesh as part of what it called a campaign to erase Islamic heritage.

Nepal's flood catastrophe and the regional response

The scale of the disaster and Pakistan's aid response show how climate-linked mountain hazards are becoming a shared regional emergency.

A glacial and mountain collapse on the China-Nepal border has killed more than a thousand people, with Nepal alone recovering 1,341 bodies and thousands still missing across the two countries. Aid workers are also confronting a secondary crisis in the shelters, with the UN estimating hundreds of births among displaced women in the coming month and dozens of them potentially needing emergency obstetric care. Pakistan has dispatched 100 tonnes of humanitarian assistance, including tents, blankets, mats, hygiene kits and medicines, to support Nepal's relief effort.

India's institutions under strain

Each story points to the same strain: public faith in India's exam system, building safety enforcement, and rule of law is being tested at once.

India's national testing system is facing a deepening crisis of public trust after a string of exam scandals, with critics debating whether reforming the agency is enough or the entire model needs to change. In Delhi, a five-storey hostel collapsed with as many as fifty people feared to have been inside at the time. In Gujarat, police publicly beat a man accused of killing his partner, an act of public parading and flogging that is not uncommon despite Supreme Court rulings against it.

Germany's far right breaks through

A far-right landslide in a former East German state signals how deep the CDU's problems have become and how mainstream the AfD's politics are turning in parts of Europe.

Germany's far-right AfD won a landslide victory in Saxony-Anhalt's state election, taking at least 44 percent of the vote in exit polls, well ahead of Chancellor Friedrich Merz's CDU, which suffered a disastrous result. The AfD's lead candidate, Ulrich Siegmund, called it a historic result, though the party fell just short of an outright majority. Siegmund also thanked Elon Musk for the party's cooperation and welcomed further support, while France's government warned that the win marked a 'serious moment' for the region.

Ukraine's peace talks stall as war grinds toward winter

Renewed US shuttle diplomacy hasn't closed the gap between Russia and Ukraine, meaning the war's costs and its strain on Western support keep compounding.

US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner held talks in Kyiv, but the meeting produced no announcement of a breakthrough between Russia and Ukraine. President Zelensky said he now expects the war to continue into winter. Analysts note the talks were rich in symbolism but that fundamental differences between Moscow and Kyiv remain unresolved.

US-Iran shadow war escalates, and Gaza displacement plans draw Muslim backlash

Two escalating fronts, direct US-Iran naval clashes and renewed displacement rhetoric on Gaza, are pulling the wider Middle East further from any settlement, with Pakistan positioning itself diplomatically on the Gaza question.

Iran's top negotiator warned of a 'faster, heavier, more painful' response if the US launches further attacks, a day after Washington said it struck Iranian oil tankers in retaliation for Tehran's attacks on US warships. The exchanges are part of a six-month conflict between the two countries that began with US raids and remains in deadlock. Separately, Pakistan and seven other Muslim-majority nations issued a joint statement condemning Israeli remarks calling for the displacement of Palestinians from Gaza, warning that such plans would undermine regional stability and violate international law.
