Pakistan's Fractured Coalition Politics

Pakistan's governing coalition is being tested on two fronts at once - a Supreme Court standoff over Imran Khan's medical treatment, and a PML-N-PPP rift over the AJK Assembly that has left parliament short of quorum.

The Supreme Court's August 18 order to move Imran Khan to Shifa Hospital for treatment remains contested: PIMS insists its CT scanner works despite reports it failed to conduct his heart scan, the government and opposition traded contempt accusations in the Senate, and the Court will hear a formal contempt petition on September 16. Khan's sister continues her court-mandated weekly jail visits, and the interior minister says the government will implement whatever the Supreme Court clarifies. At the same time, PML-N swept the AJK Assembly's speaker and deputy speaker races and nominated its own AJK prime minister, a result that has strained its coalition with the PPP. National Assembly Speaker Ayaz Sadiq acknowledged the resulting quorum problems in Islamabad, and PM Shehbaz Sharif has taken notice of lawmakers' absenteeism.

Pakistan's Energy Crunch

Fuel prices and the stalled Iran pipeline are the same energy-security squeeze playing out in different arenas, with the government now facing organized street pressure over it.

Pakistan raised petrol and diesel prices again this week, with taxes making up a large share of the retail cost, while Jamaat-i-Islami's ten-day sit-in against the petroleum levy has escalated into threats of a nationwide shutdown and a march on Islamabad. In parallel, the government kicked off a feasibility study for strategic petroleum reserves and formed a new high-powered committee to resolve the long-stalled Iran-Pakistan gas pipeline, which carries the threat of significant financial penalties if left unresolved. Together the moves point to a state trying to manage fuel costs and supply security on several fronts at once, under mounting street pressure.

Iran Under Sanctions Pressure

Washington's sanctions push on Iran is drawing in Pakistan directly, through both the stalled gas pipeline and its military chief's recent visit to Tehran.

The US is escalating 'Operation Economic Outcast' sanctions against Iran, targeting the financial networks Tehran uses to evade earlier measures, and threatening to isolate countries that keep trading with it. China, described as Iran's biggest oil buyer, has twice denounced the sanctions as illegal and vowed to take all necessary measures to protect its interests. A new report says Trump ignored warnings, including from Gabbard that killing Iran's leader could bring a hard-line successor and risk the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, before launching the Iran war. Pakistan is threading its own path through this: an Iranian official praised Pakistan's CDF Munir's recent visit as yielding highly valuable diplomatic results, even as Islamabad separately works to resolve the sanctions-threatened Iran-Pakistan gas pipeline.

Russia's Shadow Diplomacy and Europe's Response

Quiet US-Russia contact and a frontline European state hardening its defenses show two different tracks of the same relationship moving at once.

The CIA director reportedly made an unannounced trip to Moscow this week, with flight-tracking data showing a US military aircraft routed through Latvia into Russia. Separately, Sweden's armed forces are seeking to seize a Russian-owned estate near a naval base, citing the threat from Russian drones. The two events sit at opposite ends of the same relationship: quiet high-level contact between Washington and Moscow on one side, and a frontline European state hardening against perceived Russian intrusion on the other.

Trump's Use of State Power

Trade, immigration and voting rules are three separate fronts where the Trump administration is exercising state power aggressively, drawing pushback abroad and tightening control at home.

Canada has escalated its trade war with the US, announcing dollar-for-dollar retaliatory tariffs of up to 50% on goods from steel to fresh tuna. At home, the administration's rollback of protections for military families led to the deportation of another soldier's spouse, this time to Honduras. The Supreme Court has also backed a Trump administration plan that threatens mail-in voting ahead of the midterms. Three different fronts show the administration using state power aggressively, facing rising resistance abroad even as it tightens control at home.

Fragile States and Organized Crime

Weak state authority is being filled by organized violence and fraud from Haiti to Nigeria to West Africa's cybercrime networks.

A gang attack near Haiti's capital killed at least 47 people and left dozens kidnapped, with the gang leader threatening to kill hostages after setting homes alight. In Nigeria, the president ordered a manhunt after kidnappers posted video of dozens of captives held in a forest. A separate international operation led to mass arrests across West Africa for cybercrime, including dozens held in South Africa for romance and investment scams. Each case shows organized criminal networks operating in the space left open by weak state authority, from physical kidnapping-for-ransom to digital fraud.
