Pakistan's new-provinces debate

The interior minister and coalition partners are openly arguing over redrawing Pakistan's provincial map, with Punjab's chief minister's remarks on inter-provincial security triggering a backlash that exposes how raw the issue still is.

Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi is pressing for new provinces, arguing the existing four-province structure can no longer manage the population, and Tribune reporting suggests he may be pointing toward a referendum to gauge public opinion. Coalition partner Khawaja Asif calls devolution of power 'inevitable' while accusing an ally of a contradictory stance, and the PPP has rejected any division of Sindh, with Senator Raza Rabbani saying the question is not a federal matter to begin with. The debate sharpened after Punjab Chief Minister Maryam Nawaz said her province faces a greater security threat from other provinces than from neighbouring countries, since terrorism enters Punjab through provincial borders — remarks critics called chauvinistic and insulting to provinces that have borne the brunt of terrorism, with demands she withdraw them. Naqvi has said he will not back down, insisting his call for a 'reset' was not directed at any political party, while the PTI has asked why the prime minister has stayed largely out of the debate.

Balochistan: peace pledged amid rising attacks

The army chief's reassurance on Balochistan comes as attacks in the province are increasing, a recurring cycle that shapes how much control the state actually has over its most restive territory.

Field Marshal Asim Munir visited Quetta Garrison and reaffirmed that peace, prosperity and the wellbeing of Balochistan's people remain central to state efforts, a statement issued amid an uptick in attacks in the province. He said a secure environment would allow development initiatives to proceed and improve prospects for Baloch residents. No new measures or timeline were announced alongside the pledge.

Nepal's flood disaster: rescues continue

Survivors are still being pulled from Nepal's flash floods more than ten days on, showing how far behind initial rescue efforts fell in reaching everyone affected.

Rescue teams in Nepal are still finding survivors more than ten days after a flash flood disaster, including a Chinese worker recovered at the Trishuli hydropower project and a Nepali woman found in Nuwakot. A 64-year-old woman, Chandika Shrestha, was airlifted to hospital after surviving in her partially buried home for over ten days. That people are still being found alive this long afterward points to how many were initially missed or unreachable.

Ukraine war diplomacy

Trump's envoys are shuttling between Moscow and Kyiv with promises of a deal, but the negotiation is unfolding alongside, not instead of, Ukraine's own hardening break with Russia.

US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner met Vladimir Putin in Moscow, and the White House said they discussed 'substantive plans for next steps' toward ending a war now past four years old, with details due in the coming weeks. The envoys were set to travel on to Kyiv for talks with President Zelenskyy directly after the Moscow meeting. Separately, Odesa, Ukraine's third-largest and majority Russian-speaking city, is deciding this week whether to ban Russian-language content in music and literature in public. Together, the two stories show a war being negotiated at the top while, inside Ukraine, the cultural separation from Russia keeps hardening.

Middle East: US-Iran clash, Gaza and the West Bank

Three separate fronts — US-Iran naval confrontation, Gaza's future, and West Bank violence — are all escalating at once, and Washington is trying to manage all three without being seen to lose control of any of them.

The US and Iran traded retaliatory ship attacks, with Washington saying it hit three Iranian oil tankers and Tehran saying it targeted three US-linked vessels. In Gaza, the US ambassador to Israel said there was no active plan to force Palestinians out of the territory, a statement made after Israeli ministers had floated ideas for expelling Gaza's roughly two million residents. In the West Bank, Israeli journalist Gideon Levy said attacks by Israeli occupiers amount to ethnic cleansing, citing a rise in recorded attacks from 1,291 in 2023 to 1,835 in 2025, with 761 already logged in early 2026. Each story marks a different front of the same widening confrontation involving Israel, the Palestinians and Iran.

Latin America: violence and shifting alliances

Insurgent and institutional violence keeps testing state control across the region, while Washington's backing increasingly follows personal loyalty to individual leaders rather than fixed alliances.

Separately, Donald Trump signalled he might back Argentine ally Javier Milei's push to reclaim the Falkland Islands, a gesture read as pique at the UK.
