Pakistan under pressure: politics, courts, and economy

It shows Islamabad managing street and provincial pressure over prices and federalism at the same time as it tries to project economic stability to investors and rating agencies.

Jamaat-i-Islami has given the government until September 10 to withdraw the petroleum levy or face a long march on Islamabad, and the government responded not by cutting fuel prices but by raising them again. The Sindh Assembly has sent Islamabad a resolution rejecting any division of the province, while a judicial commission probing an alleged police negligence case was disrupted when the provincial advocate general objected to the victim's family's lawyer questioning a witness. The Federal Constitutional Court has ruled that religious freedom is not absolute, upholding a ban on a 23-volume Ahmadi publication. Against this backdrop, the government is also pushing an economic-diplomacy counter-narrative: dollar inflows through exchange companies are up 30 percent year-on-year, PM Shehbaz is courting business leaders on export-led growth and launching a Pakistan-Africa Economic Council, and Moody's says Pakistan is more resilient to external shocks than in 2022, though still fragile.

Pakistan's regional security commitments under strain

Pakistan's defence pact with Saudi Arabia could pull Islamabad into a widening Gulf-Iran confrontation just as pressure builds over how to handle the Taliban on its western border.

Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia injured 73 people and set oil facilities on fire, and Riyadh has vowed to respond. Pakistan's defence minister says the Makkah mutual defence pact will become operative if the aggression against Saudi Arabia continues, and PM Shehbaz has separately backed Saudi Arabia's right to defend its territory. Iran's Revolutionary Guard says it has captured a US underwater drone and shot down a US aircraft over the Strait of Hormuz, another flashpoint in the same theatre. Piracy off Somalia has returned to levels unseen in a decade, linked partly to disruption from the US-Israeli war against Iran, while separately Human Rights Watch is warning against normalising ties with the Taliban without human rights guarantees, five years into their rule over Afghanistan.

UK-Israel rupture over West Bank sanctions

A close Western ally imposing sanctions over settler violence and Gaza marks a real crack in the international backing Israel has relied on.

Britain has imposed sanctions on goods from Israeli settlements in the West Bank, with Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband accusing settlers of 'ethnic cleansing' and calling the situation a 'moral emergency', pointing to what he called mounting evidence of war crimes in Gaza. Israel responded by shutting the UK consulate in East Jerusalem. BBC's diplomatic correspondent frames this as pushing British-Israeli relations to their lowest point in decades.

Ukraine: war, corruption and Western resolve

Kyiv is under pressure on three fronts at once - Russian strikes, a corruption scandal reaching its own senior ranks, and the need to keep Western backers convinced the war effort is worth continued funding.

A Russian drone hit a TV channel building in Kyiv, killing five, which Zelensky called 'a new page in Russian degradation.' Ukraine's prosecutor general Ruslan Kravchenko has resigned after anti-corruption investigators raided his office over allegations that officials under him were involved in large-scale money laundering, a scandal that threatens to embarrass Zelenskyy. Separately, a senior US defence official told European countries they 'must not slacken' on remilitarisation and support for Ukraine, part of a push to secure 300 Patriot interceptors for Kyiv, as the UK committed £100m for Ukraine's air defences.

US-Canada trade war escalates

A trade war between two of the world's most integrated economies signals how far protectionism has become normal policy rather than a negotiating threat.

The US has imposed an import ban on Canadian alcohol, motorbikes and other goods, the latest move in a long-running trade war. Canada's retaliatory tariffs on American goods have now taken effect too, following Trump's earlier announcement of 50 percent tariffs on Canadian goods, escalating the dispute between the two countries.

Twenty-five years after 9/11: an unfinished reckoning

The anniversary is surfacing both an unresolved legal case and a real shift in how Americans define the terrorist threat, from foreign to domestic.

As the 25th anniversary of September 11 approaches, families of the bereaved say the wait for the alleged mastermind of the attacks to face trial continues to weigh on them. Newly released documents show toxins in the air around Ground Zero lingered for months after the attacks even though officials told New Yorkers it was safe, according to Mamdani. A new poll finds 61 percent of Americans now see domestic extremists as a bigger threat than foreign attacks, a sharp rise from a decade ago.
