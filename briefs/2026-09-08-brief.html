Pakistan's rising political confrontation

This is the political temperature Pakistan is carrying into a September 27 showdown over redrawing the federation's map, unfolding alongside a string of separate cases testing how much dissent the state will tolerate.

Pakistan's fight over creating new provinces is escalating: KP Chief Minister Sohail Afridi and PTI's Barrister Gohar say they won't back new provinces without Imran Khan's approval and broad consensus, while PPP's Sharjeel Memon has dismissed the objectors as holding illegitimate 'Form 47' mandates. Afridi is urging people to join a September 27 protest he says will shut down Karachi, even as the Islamabad High Court hears a petition to block PTI's planned march on the grounds it would disrupt daily life. The same period saw several separate cases of the state moving against dissent: police briefly detained relatives of sailors held hostage by Somali pirates during a sit-in, a former Quetta police surgeon was remanded over alleged anti-state remarks, a PTI leader's detention in the Jinnah House attack case was extended, and a court granted bail to 96 people held over a protest in Azad Kashmir. A judicial commission also summoned officials over the disputed Mir Raza Ali murder case, in which the victim's family has accused police of a cover-up.

Pakistan's institutions: courts, regulators, and rising costs

While the provinces fight dominates the headlines, courts, regulators and legislators are quietly making decisions on rights, prices and law enforcement that will outlast this news cycle.

The Supreme Court upheld a Sindh High Court order requiring Pakistan Bait-ul-Mal to pay a monthly stipend to Hindu girls until marriage, with the Chief Justice saying courts may look beyond narrow legal questions to address the underlying problem. The power regulator Nepra approved new uniform charges for electricity distribution companies and cleared the way for the country's first competitive open-access power auction, while the federal government identified seven Gilgit-Baltistan roads to take over and rehabilitate for tourism. The government also raised petrol and diesel prices again, and Jamaat-i-Islami's first round of talks with the government on scrapping the petroleum levy ended with the party submitting six proposals ahead of a second round. Separately, a Senate committee passed a bill proposing the death penalty for acid attacks alongside tougher penalties for other offences, and Pakistan's Interpol bureau reported issuing hundreds of red notices and repatriating dozens of fugitives over the past seven months.

Germany's far-right breakthrough

A near-doubling of the AfD's vote share in a German state tests whether the political 'firewall' against governing with the far right can hold, with implications for the EU's cohesion.

The AfD scored a big win in the Saxony-Anhalt state election, nearly doubling its previous result and falling just three seats short of an outright majority. Chancellor Friedrich Merz called the result a shock that shook his conservative CDU 'to its very foundations' and said Germany 'cannot simply move past it,' while vowing to stay in office. AfD's leadership said 'democracy demands' mainstream parties work with them, directly testing the firewall under which German parties have refused to govern alongside the far right. Commentary framed the AfD's rise as rooted in mainstream parties' own policy failures and years of gradual normalisation of far-right ideas, rather than a sudden shift in public opinion.

Russia's war grinds on and widens

Russia has launched round-the-clock strikes on Ukraine, pushing air raid alerts in Kyiv to a record high and straining schools, businesses and public transport. Russia and North Korea opened their first road bridge across their shared border, the latest sign of deepening ties between the two countries since the war began.
